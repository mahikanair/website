Hi, I'm Mahika Nair.

I’m a machine learning and AI engineer with a background in mathematics and chemistry and a passion for using computational approaches to tackle challenging research problems at the intersection of AI and the life sciences.

I first developed this interest during my BSc (Hons.) in Mathematics, with a Minor in Chemistry, and continued through a Postgraduate Diploma in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning. I became particularly interested in how computational methods and AI can be applied to problems in biomedical and biochemical domains, exploring this through research including MRI radiomics for breast cancer tumour characterisation and computational work on nanoporous 2D materials published in the Journal of the American Chemical Society.

I recently completed my MSc in Data Science at the University of Edinburgh, where my dissertation focused on developing a deep-learning surrogate model for Self-Consistent Field Theory simulations of protein adsorption. The project combined deep learning and protein science to replace computationally intensive simulations with a model capable of making predictions approximately 200× faster.

Before my MSc, I worked as an Associate AI Engineer at Predli, building and improving scalable AI systems and taking machine learning solutions from development into production. I’m now looking to combine this industry experience with my research background to develop AI and ML approaches for scientific discovery, particularly in areas such as protein science, drug discovery, and computational biology.

Satellite Crop Classification

Trained the lightweight pixel-based Vision Transformer PRESTO on time-series multimodal Sentinel-2 satellite imagery for crop classification across Indian agricultural regions, achieving an F1-score of 85.5% in Punjab.

Further pretrained PRESTO using higher-resolution Planet satellite imagery, adapting the model from 10 m to 4 m resolution and building a high-resolution representation of Indian geographical contexts before downstream crop-classification fine-tuning.
